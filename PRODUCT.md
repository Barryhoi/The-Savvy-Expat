# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

Primary users are affluent American retirees and veterans relocating to the Philippines, typically with $4,000-$10,000+/month in retirement or investment income. Evidenced by client-story flagships (a 71-year-old Air Force veteran, a Wall Street lawyer with a $10,000 budget, veterans using the retirement visa), "retire comfortably" framing throughout the newsletter, and testimonial voices skewing older and financially established. The newsletter archive also reaches a wider top-of-funnel audience (broader remote-income earners, prospective expats still deciding), but the paid coaching offer itself is built around this affluent retiree/veteran segment.

## Product Purpose

The Savvy Expat is a done-for-you relocation concierge service that gets Americans moved to the Philippines (specifically BGC/Metro Manila) without the trial-and-error, wasted capital, or coordination overhead of doing it alone or hiring multiple disconnected agencies. Success means a client is fully relocated and settled — housing, banking, visa, and daily life — with Evan and his team handling the logistics.

## Positioning

"Your all-in-one Expat Partner" — a single coordinated partner with 6+ years of on-the-ground experience and an existing local network (landlords, banks, visa processors, lifestyle contacts), versus the two alternatives clients otherwise face: doing it themselves (uncertain, error-prone, wastes time/capital) or hiring multiple disconnected agencies (messy, expensive, nobody owns the whole picture). The mechanism a competitor can't easily copy is Evan's personal network and hands-on 24/7 access, not just information — the newsletter/YouTube content is top-of-funnel proof of expertise, the paid service is the actual relocation execution.

## Operating Context

- Service area: BGC (Bonifacio Global City) / Metro Manila-focused. Comparisons, cost breakdowns, and case studies center on BGC specifically rather than the Philippines broadly.
- Engagement starts with a "Book A Call" CTA — no price is published on the site; pricing is a custom quote determined after the discovery call.
- Companion content channels: a weekly newsletter (beehiiv, 56+ issues archived on this site) and a YouTube channel (64K+ subscribers) that build trust and feed the coaching funnel; the free newsletter/subscribe page and the paid coaching VSL are two different offers on the same site.
- Core service scope: finding/securing rental housing, opening a local bank account, visa filing and documentation, lifestyle setup (restaurants, gyms, country clubs, routine), SIM cards and utilities, plus ongoing 24/7 access to Evan and his team.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Confirmed functionality on this site: VSL homepage for the coaching offer (`/`), full newsletter archive with sectioned browse (`/newsletter`), a minimal email-capture squeeze page (`/subscribe`), and a video/watch page (`/watch`).
- Pricing model is explicitly undecided/unpublished by design — do not invent a price or package tier anywhere on the site.
- Newsletter content is written and bylined by Evan Lorezca; do not attribute it to a generic "team" voice.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: "The Savvy Expat." Founder/face of the brand: Evan Lorezca.
- Voice: direct, specific, numbers-driven (real budgets, real costs, no "sugarcoating" — this phrasing recurs across newsletter titles).

## Evidence on Hand

- Stats used on the homepage: 200+ Expats Relocated, 97+ Five Star Reviews, 6+ Years Living In The Philippines, 64K+ Subscribers On YouTube.
- Six video client-testimonial embeds and six written testimonial quotes (Ralph, Mart, Michael, Bruce, Anthony, Bryan) with real client photos, already in use on the homepage — do not invent additional testimonials.
- 56+ published newsletter issues (beehiiv), read in full during the newsletter-page redesign and organized into six real categories (Client Stories, Cost of Living & Money, Lifestyle & Guides, Visas & Residency, Housing & Real Estate, Healthcare).
- No pricing, guarantee terms, or refund policy is published anywhere on the current site — treat as a stated absence, not a gap to fill in.

## Product Principles

1. Specificity over hype — real numbers (budgets, costs, timelines) beat vague promises; this is the newsletter's and the site's core credibility mechanism.
2. One coordinated partner, not a pile of information — the value is Evan doing the coordination and having the local network, not just teaching the client what to do.
3. Content earns the sale — the newsletter/YouTube are proof-of-expertise top-of-funnel assets that feed the coaching offer; they are not the product itself.
4. BGC-specific credibility — grounding claims in one specific place (not "the Philippines" generically) is what makes the cost/lifestyle comparisons believable.
